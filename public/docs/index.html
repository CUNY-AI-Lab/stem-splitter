Stem Splitter Documentation

Stem Splitter separates a recording into musical layers so students can hear how each part contributes to the song. Use the mixer to compare those layers, then pin a note to the passage that supports your interpretation.

About Stem Splitter

Stem Splitter gives music students a browser mixer for close listening. The CUNY AI Lab hosts the tool, which uses Demucs to separate a recording into stems such as vocals, drums, and bass. The stems play together so students can mute one part or hear a few parts on their own. A timestamped note connects a student's description to the moment they heard.

The Critical AI Literacy T(h)inkering track developed the tool for students who may have no formal music background. Listening Guy adds an AI guide and chat to each recording. It can operate the mixer and suggest a listening question, but it cannot hear the audio. Students use the stems to check its claims.

Getting Started

Getting Started explains how to join a course, sign in, and make a first split. Once the recording is ready, Splitter Basics helps you find it in the Session Rack and understand its status.

Listen & Analyze

Start with The Mixer to hear a stem alone, slow a passage, or set a loop. Use Notes to record what changed and mark the time. The Listening Guy page explains how the guide uses those notes and how to check its answers. When you want to keep or share the audio, Export & Sharing covers downloads, public links, and class folders.

Teach & Share

Instructors need a course in the Lab's admission system to use the classroom features. Class Workspace explains how students assign work to that course and how folders control access. Live Sessions shows how to lead a shared listening session on students' devices. The Instructor Console lets you give Listening Guy teaching instructions.

Student Onboarding offers a first-week plan, and Teaching Tips connects classroom questions to mixer controls. For a task you can use in one meeting, choose from Sample Activities. The longer Use Cases show how to develop those exercises across a music survey course.

Supporting Resources

Privacy & Limits explains what instructors can see, when files expire, and how daily allowances work. The Lab's Sandbox Docs provide a companion guide to its Sandbox. Contact AI Lab support for access questions, or use Report a bug in the app footer to report a bug.
