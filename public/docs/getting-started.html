Getting Started

Sign in with CUNY Login for Personal work or a class you belong to, then make a first split. A familiar recording gives you a useful starting point because you can compare the stems with a song you already know.

Get Access

Students reach Stem Splitter through an instructor's course in the CUNY AI Lab. Instructors first request a course through the Lab's course application. After the Lab approves and activates the course, Stem Splitter appears under My classes in the Lab portal.

Instructors invite students through the Lab's class invitation. Students accept while the invitation window is open, then open Stem Splitter from My classes or sign in at stem-splitter.ailab-452.workers.dev. The app recognizes each member's course role. Lab members who have no course can also sign in and keep personal splits, which stay private to their accounts.

First Sign-In

Use the same CUNY Login you use for other CUNY services. Choose CUNY Login below the CUNY AI Lab logo, then complete any password or multi-factor prompt through CUNY Login. Use a recent browser with audio playback, such as Chrome, Firefox, Safari, or Edge. Headphones help when you share a room or need to hear a quiet part.

After sign-in, the menu shows Splitter, Workspace, and Account. Account contains Settings and Dashboard. Dashboard shows your allowances; Settings lets you save Personal Listening Guy instructions. Class tools appear only when your current class role permits them. The menu stays hidden while you are signed out or using a guest session.

Your First Split

Leave Share with set to Personal for your first try. You can learn the controls with a private recording before assigning new work to a course. Class Workspace explains the course policy and its acknowledgment.

Drop an audio file onto Insert Track, or click the drop zone to browse. The app accepts MP3, WAV, FLAC, M4A, OGG, and AIFF files up to 100 MB. The search bar sits directly below the upload area. Enter a song, artist, or genre, such as jazz or queen rock, select an available audio result, choose 2, 4, or 6 stems, and press Fetch. When the empty search bar shows a suggested song, press Tab on a keyboard or double-tap the bar on a phone to take it. The search queries the available catalogs together; there is no source selector or accordion. Search combines audio catalogs in one list. Each file keeps its source, creator, and license credits with the recording and its exports. MP3 previews are labeled when the source offers a converted version. Files must pass the app's rights, duration, format, and size checks. Use music you are entitled to use for class.

If one audio source is unavailable, results from the others remain usable. Try another recording or upload an audio file when a search has no available results.

Choose the available 2-, 4-, or 6-stem option. A model output name is not a verified inventory of the instruments audible in that file. Listen to each stem before relying on its label; instrument identity and isolation quality are not guaranteed by the number of outputs.

The new recording appears in the Session Rack with a status badge. Wait for READY, then press play to hear the stems together. The separation service processes the audio on a GPU, and a typical song may take a minute or two. You can leave the page and return to check its progress.

Try Solo on the bass and listen for one minute. Clear the solo, replay the same minute with every stem, and notice how the bass relates to the other instruments. Then mute the bass and describe what changed. The Splitter Basics page explains the rack and the controls around the mixer.

Keep Listening

The Listening Guy guide offers questions you can test against the stems. Start with a claim about one instrument, hear that instrument alone, and pin a note at a passage that supports or challenges the claim.

Your personal work stays private to your account. When you create a course split, the policy you acknowledge determines what your instructor can review. The app deletes uploaded audio and stems after 90 days; Privacy & Limits explains retention and sharing in detail.
